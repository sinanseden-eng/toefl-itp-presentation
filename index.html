📋 Slide 1 • Overview

What is the TOEFL ITP® Level 1 Exam?

The TOEFL ITP® Level 1 evaluates academic English proficiency for non-native speakers in university settings.

  • Institutional Purpose: Used by universities for class placement, progress monitoring, program exit requirements, and scholarship selection.
  • Validity: Scores are valid for 2 years and benchmarked against international standards (CEFR A2 to C1).
  • Focus: Evaluates general academic English skills without requiring specialized subject knowledge.

💡 TOEFL ITP vs. TOEFL iBT

TOEFL ITP is an institutional paper/digital test for placement & progress. TOEFL iBT is a computer test for international admissions with direct speaking/writing tasks.

Section 1: Listening Comprehension

Measures your ability to understand spoken English in university settings.

  • Part A: 30 Short Conversations — Campus life exchanges
  • Part B: 2 Extended Conversations — Academic discussions
  • Part C: 3 Short Talks — University-level mini-lectures

⏱️ Key Rule

Audio plays ONLY ONCE. Notes may be taken in the test book.

👨‍🏫 Teacher Deep-Dive • Listening
🎧

Listening Subskills & Tested Competencies

  • 1. Main Idea & Purpose: Identifying the central theme or reason for a conversation/lecture.
  • 2. Direct Detail Recognition: Catching explicitly stated facts, dates, causes, and results.
  • 3. Implied Meaning & Inferences: Reading between the lines to deduce speaker mood, attitude, or unstated intention.
  • 4. Idiomatic & Campus Language: Recognizing common idioms, phrasal verbs, and conversational expressions used in North American higher education.
  • 5. Structural & Transition Cues: Following organizational markers in mini-lectures (e.g., cause/effect, contrast, chronology).
💡 Teacher Tip: Remind students that audio tracks are played ONLY ONCE. Note-taking is allowed and encouraged! Practice capturing key details while listening.
Sample Part A Item: (Audio) Woman: "Shall I lock up the lab before I go home?"
Man: "Don't bother. I'm not leaving for a while."
Question: What will the man probably do? ▶ Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: Lock the lab later. Explanation: "Don't bother" means the woman doesn't need to do it now because the man is staying and will lock it when he leaves.

✏️

Section 2: Structure & Written Expression

Tests recognition of standard written academic English without requiring essay writing.

Structure (15 Qs)

Select the phrase that correctly completes the sentence.

Written Expression (25 Qs)

Identify the underlined word or phrase containing an error.

⏱️ Pacing Strategy

Spend no more than ~37 seconds per item (25 mins total).

👨‍🏫 Teacher Deep-Dive • Structure
✏️

Grammatical Subskills & Error Types

  • 1. Clause Structure & Connectors: Mastering independent and dependent clauses, coordinate conjunctions (and, but), and subordinate clauses (because, although, which).
  • 2. Subject-Verb Agreement: Matching singular/plural subjects with verbs across complex, prepositional, or inverted sentences.
  • 3. Verb Forms & Tenses: Correctly identifying past/present participles, modal verbs, passive voice, and perfect tenses.
  • 4. Parallelism & Word Order: Ensuring lists and paired conjunctions (both...and, neither...nor) share identical grammatical structures.
  • 5. Word Form & Part of Speech Confusion: Spotting incorrect usage of nouns vs. verbs vs. adjectives vs. adverbs (e.g., confusing "quick" for "quickly").
💡 Teacher Tip: Pacing is key! Students have only ~37 seconds per question. Teach them to quickly identify the error type and eliminate wrong choices systematically.
Sample Written Expression Item: "Only those insects with high (A) developed, multilensed (B) eyes have (C) good color (D) vision." ▶ Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: (A) high Explanation: The adverb "highly" is required to modify the adjective "developed" (highly developed).
📖 Slide 6 • Reading
📖

Section 3: Reading Comprehension

Tests comprehension of academic texts typical of university textbooks.

  • Format: 5 passages (~250–350 words each) | 50 Questions | 55 Minutes
  • Topics: Science, History, Social Sciences, and the Arts

⏱️ Pacing Tip

Spend approximately 11 minutes per passage.

👨‍🏫 Teacher Deep-Dive • Reading
📖

Reading Comprehension Subskills

  • 1. Main Idea & Passage Purpose: Locating topic sentences and synthesizing the overall thesis of a passage.
  • 2. Stated Details & Negative Facts: Finding explicit facts or identifying which statement is NOT mentioned ("ALL of the following are true EXCEPT...").
  • 3. Inferences & Logical Deduction: Drawing reasonable conclusions based on facts provided in the text.
  • 4. Vocabulary in Context: Contextually defining academic terms and multiple-meaning words as used in the passage.
  • 5. Pronoun Referents: Tracing pronouns (it, they, these, which) back to their specific antecedent nouns.
💡 Teacher Tip: Encourage students to skim the passage first for structure, then scan for targeted facts when answering. Teach them to read questions before the passage to know what to look for.
Sample Question Type: Pronoun Referent "...Scientists examined the fossils. They revealed unique traits..."
Question: The word "They" in line 2 refers to: ▶ Show Answer & Explanation
Correct Answer: fossils Explanation: "They" is a plural pronoun referring back to the nearest logical plural noun antecedent, "fossils."
🏆 Slide 7 • Strategy

Proven Test-Taking Strategies

  • Pace Yourself: Work quickly and keep moving. Don't get stuck on one difficult item.
  • Bubble Every Item: Wrong answers are not penalized—always guess if time is running out.
  • Practice Note-Taking: Write notes in your test book during Listening to remember key details.
  • Daily Preparation: Read academic articles and listen to university lectures to build stamina.
