TOEFL ITP® LEVEL 1 GUIDE

Prep is course — a seven-card tour through format, scoring and strategy. Section cards flip open: students see the overview, teachers flip to the subskill deep-dive.

or press → / Enter · flip cards with F

ESSENTIALS

A paper-based, all-multiple-choice test of academic English — used for placement, scholarships and exchange screening.

  • Three sections, 140 questions, about 115 minutes.
  • Answers go on a separate answer sheet — practice bubbling.
  • No speaking or writing tasks; scores valid for two years.

FORMAT

Listening Comprehension50 Q35 min
Structure & Written Expression40 Q25 min
Reading Comprehension50 Q55 min
ITP vs iBT: ITP is paper-based and set by your institution; the iBT adds speaking & writing online.

SCORING

Each section scores 31–68; the total (310–677) is the sum × 10 ÷ 3.

620–677 · C1543–617 · B2460–542 · B1310–459 · A2
No penalty for wrong answers — an educated guess always beats a blank.

LISTENING

Section 150 questions~35 min
  • Part A: 30 short conversations · B: 2 extended conversations · C: 3 academic talks.
  • Goal: understand spoken English in university settings — lectures, office hours, campus life.
Teacher View · Deep-Dive Skill Breakdown

Listening Subskills & Tested Competencies

  1. Main Idea & Purpose: identifying the central theme or reason for a conversation or lecture.
  2. Direct Detail Recognition: catching explicitly stated facts, dates, causes and results.
  3. Implied Meaning & Inference: reading between the lines for speaker mood, attitude or unstated intention.
  4. Idiomatic & Campus Language: idioms, phrasal verbs and expressions of North American higher education.
  5. Structural & Transition Cues: following organization in mini-lectures (cause/effect, contrast, chronology).
Teacher tip: audio plays only once — and note-taking in the test book is allowed. Drill both habits early.
Sample Part A item. (Audio) Woman: “Shall I lock up the lab before I go home?” Man: “Don’t bother. I’m not leaving for a while.” — What will the man probably do?
Answer: lock the lab later. “Don’t bother” signals the woman needn’t act now — the man stays and will lock up when he leaves.

STRUCTURE

Section 240 questions25 min
  • Structure (15): choose the phrase that correctly completes each sentence.
  • Written Expression (25): spot the one underlined error.
  • Goal: standard written academic English — no essay required.
Teacher View · Deep-Dive Skill Breakdown

Grammatical Subskills & Error Types

  1. Clause Structure & Connectors: independent/dependent clauses; coordinate (and, but) and subordinate (because, although, which) conjunctions.
  2. Subject–Verb Agreement: matching number across prepositional, inverted or interrupted subjects.
  3. Verb Forms & Tenses: participles, modals, passive voice and perfect tenses.
  4. Parallelism & Word Order: paired conjunctions (both…and, neither…nor) demand identical structures.
  5. Word-Form Confusion: noun vs verb vs adjective vs adverb — e.g. quick used where quickly is required.
Teacher tip: pacing is key — only ~37 seconds per item. Train a quick “subject first, then verb” scan.
Sample Written Expression item. “Only those insects with high (A) developed, multilensed (B) eyes have (C) good color (D) vision.”
Answer: (A) high. The adverb highly must modify the adjective “developed” (highly developed).

READING

Section 350 questions55 min
  • Format: 5 academic passages, ~250–350 words each.
  • Topics: science, history, social sciences and the arts.
  • Goal: read and analyze college-level texts (~11 min per passage).
Teacher View · Deep-Dive Skill Breakdown

Reading Comprehension Subskills

  1. Main Idea & Passage Purpose: locating topic sentences and synthesizing the overall thesis.
  2. Stated Details & Negative Facts: explicit facts, plus “ALL of the following are true EXCEPT…” items.
  3. Inference & Logical Deduction: reasonable conclusions drawn from stated facts.
  4. Vocabulary in Context: defining academic and multiple-meaning words as used in the passage.
  5. Pronoun Referents: tracing it / they / these / which back to their antecedent nouns.
Teacher tip: skim first for structure, then scan for targeted facts while answering — never read twice for pleasure.
Sample pronoun-referent item. “…Scientists examined the fossils. They revealed unique traits…” — “They” refers to:
Answer: fossils. The plural pronoun points to the nearest logical plural antecedent, “fossils.”

STRATEGY

  • Rehearse with timed, paper-format practice tests.
  • Wear a watch — phones stay away; budget per section.
  • Answer every question; blanks score zero, guesses can't.
  • Sleep well, arrive early, bring ID and two pencils.
You're set — know the format, trust your pacing, and let consistent practice finish the job.
